Use of Technology The following artifacts provide evidence of using technology to support a TBL lesson introducing the AI Assessment Scale. Moodle was used to provide learners with curated pre-class resources, while LAMS was used to sequence team formation, readiness assurance activities, immediate feedback, application exercises, and peer review. The screenshots below show both the student and instructor perspectives and demonstrate how LAMS supports progression through the TBL learning cycle. The lesson was designed to move learners from foundational understanding toward application. The pre- class resources introduce the AI Assessment Scale, while the iRAT and tRAT assess learners' understanding of key concepts. The application exercises then require teams to apply that knowledge to authentic assessment-design decisions. The technology supports this progression by sequencing activities, providing immediate feedback, making team thinking visible, and creating opportunities for discussion and peer review. The selected screens below represent the six functions most relevant to the learning design: preparation, team formation, iRAT data, tRAT immediate feedback, burning questions, and application/peer review. Together, they show how LAMS sequences the TBL cycle and makes individual and team thinking visible. Repeated question screens and transition gates have been omitted because they demonstrate interface progression rather than additional learning.

Overall Evaluation Developing this sequence helped me better understand how technology can support the structure and ﬂow of a TBL lesson. LAMS brings team formation, readiness assurance, immediate feedback, application exercises, and peer review together within a single sequence. Features such as conﬁdence ratings, scratch-off responses, activity reports, burning questions, and the virtual gallery walk create opportunities for discussion, feedback, and learner engagement throughout the lesson. The process highlighted some practical considerations and concerns. Facilitators have their hands full: they need to manage gates, monitor learner progress, review activity data, and maintain momentum as learners move between activities. From the learner perspective, waiting at multiple gates has the potential to disrupt the ﬂow of the learner experience if those transitions are not managed well. The effectiveness of the platform therefore depends not only on how the lesson is designed but also on how conﬁdently it is facilitated. Overall, LAMS supports the phases of TBL and provides useful information about both individual and team learning. At the same time, the experience reinforced that technology should support the learning design rather than drive it.
