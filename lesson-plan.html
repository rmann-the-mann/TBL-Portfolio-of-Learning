Lesson Plan Introduction to the AI Assessment Scale BOPPPS Lesson Planning Model This lesson combines the TBL process with the BOPPPS lesson-planning framework used at SAIT (Instructional Skills Workshop International Advisory Committee, 2006). The Bridge-In introduces the signiﬁcant problem, the iRAT and tRAT provide readiness assurance, and the participatory learning phase includes two team application exercises followed by reporting and debrieﬁng. Bridge In (5 minutes) • Put ﬁve activities on the screen: Brainstorm | Outline | Draft | Revise | Generate ﬁnal submission • Ask the class and allow for discussion: “Where would you draw the line for your students?” • Then ask the class: “Would you draw that line in the same place for every assessment you teach?” Learning Outcomes • Identify the ﬁve levels of the AI Assessment Scale and the type of AI use permitted at each level. • Explain why a spectrum-based framework is more effective than a binary allowed/not-allowed approach to AI in assessment. • Apply the three guiding questions instructors should ask when determining appropriate AI use for a given assessment. Pre-Assessment (iRAT 5 minutes and tRAT 8 minutes) • iRAT and tRAT (see Moodle for access to the LAMS system) Participatory Learning (32 minutes) Application Exercise 1: What Level is This? (12 minutes) Teams are given the following four assessment descriptions. For each assessment, teams must agree on the single most appropriate AI Assessment Scale level (1-5) and be prepared to defend their choice.

Teams report their selected level simultaneously using the LAMS reporting function. The instructor compares the responses and invites teams with different choices to explain and defend their reasoning before providing clariﬁcation. Application Exercise 2: How would You Say It? (12 minutes) Teams need to write a single syllabus-ready AI use statement for the provided assessment. Teams submit one syllabus-ready statement through LAMS and participate in a virtual gallery walk. Participants review the statements produced by other teams and vote or provide feedback based on clarity, completeness, and alignment with the selected AIAS level. Gallery Walk and peer review (8 minutes) For both activities, use Moodle to access the LAMS system which has the activities. Post Assessment Learning is assessed through the decisions and justiﬁcations provided during both application exercises. In Application Exercise 1, teams select and defend an appropriate AIAS level for each assessment. In Application Exercise 2, teams demonstrate their ability to communicate an AI-use decision by creating a syllabus-ready statement. Team responses, whole-class discussion, and peer feedback provide evidence of whether the learning outcomes have been achieved. Summary (5 minutes) • Returning to the initial question: Would you draw the line in the same place for every assessment you teach? o After working with the AIAS, how would you answer that question now? • Reinforce that the appropriate level of AI use depends on what students are expected to learn and demonstrate, not simply on whether an instructor is comfortable with AI. • Conclude with: The goal of the AIAS isn't to decide whether AI is allowed. It's to intentionally decide where, how, and why AI should be used in an assessment, and to communicate that decision clearly to students.
